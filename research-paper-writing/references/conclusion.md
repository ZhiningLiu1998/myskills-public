# Conclusion

The conclusion reminds the reader what the paper showed and where it stops. It
should not introduce new claims.

## Structure

1. The problem and the core idea, in one or two sentences.
2. The strongest evidence.
3. The insight or practical consequence, if the paper has one.
4. Limitations and a concrete next step.

## Limitations

Choose limitations that describe the scope of the work: the data it was tested
on, its assumptions, or the sensors and settings it needs. For example, "We
evaluate only on short sequences" or "The method assumes a calibrated camera."

Distinguish a scope limit from a defect. A scope limit leaves the method
competitive within its setting. A defect, such as losing to baselines on a key
metric, belongs in the analysis, and the paper's claims should reflect it.

## Template

```text
This paper addresses [problem] by [core idea]. Experiments on [settings] show
[main result]. The method currently [scope limit]; extending it to [setting] is
a natural next step.
```
